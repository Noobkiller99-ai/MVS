Our interactions with self-help group members and women in the communities reinforced what we first saw in Chandpur. Entrepreneurial skills already exist, but turning them into regular income is the hard part. Access to markets shapes what a skill can earn, and collective institutions like SHGs give individual effort scale. Our reading of the path is from skills, through collective institutions and market linkage, to a sustained livelihood. The learning we carried forward is that skill development alone may not be enough without market access and economic opportunity.
