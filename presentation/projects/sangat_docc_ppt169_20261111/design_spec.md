## III. Visual Theme

### Theme Style

- **Mode**: narrative
- **Visual style**: custom
- **Visual Style References**: photo-editorial, editorial
- **Visual Style Behavior**: Photo-led editorial pages where real field photographs are large, well-cropped fields (edge bleeds, diptychs, triptychs, vertical portrait columns) and type sits in calm ivory columns beside them; brand chapters switch to a brand-book register (forest fields, generous ivory, gold hairlines, swatches, product mock-ups). Editorial serif headlines in sentence case with an italic second clause, small tracked sans kickers naming the act, thin gold rules and act numbering; flat surfaces with depth only from scrims over photographs and one soft shadow on floating product mock-ups. Photo placeholders are designed frames (tinted ground, corner crop marks, small label) composed exactly where the real photo will sit.
- **Theme**: The journey from field to brand — Act I entering the ecosystem, Act II learning from the ground, Act III discovering the market, Act IV building Sangat, Act V a development pathway. Cross-page motif: a thin muted-gold rule plus act kicker (e.g. "ACT II · LEARNING FROM THE GROUND") and a small slide counter, recurring on every content page; the Sangat landscape artwork opens and closes the deck.
- **Tone**: Respectful, human-centred, confident and evidence-honest; never a poverty lens, never an advertisement.

## V. Layout Principles

### Deck-wide Direction

- **Hierarchy direction**: Kicker → serif headline (takeaway sentence) → photo field or diagram → short supporting copy → caption/source.
- **Composition tendency**: Asymmetric magazine splits (photo 40–55% on field pages), diptychs and triptychs for multi-photo moments, page-field diagrams for analysis, brand-book grids for identity pages; avoid repeated equal card grids.
- **Cross-page continuity**: Act kicker + gold hairline + slide counter on content pages; forest field reserved for cover, act-opening emphasis and closing; landscape art bookends the deck.
- **Spacing posture**: Variable — open on breathing/photo pages, organised on dense analysis pages.
- **Spacing anchors**: page margin 56 · block gap 24 · column gutter 32 · corner radius 4 · body leading 1.5

## IX. Content Outline

### Part 1: Act I — Entering the development ecosystem

#### Slide 01 - Cover

- **Audience move**: Panel arrives expecting a brand pitch → expects a field-to-brand journey with a premium outcome
- **Relationships**: none
- **Title**: SANGAT — Launching a Sustainable Basmati Rice Brand for MVS
- **Core message**: A DoCC journey from rural fields to a proposed premium basmati brand.
- **Content**: Sangat logo (reversed) · project title · tagline Sanjhi Soch, Sunehra Kal · Ashish Satpathy | Janardhan Reddy Bogireddy · SPJIMR | Development of Corporate Citizenship | 2026 · in partnership with Manav Vikas Sansthan
- **Cover impact**: Forest field, landscape art rising from the bottom edge, logo as the opening mark.
- **Images**: logo_primary_reversed.png; art_landscape.png

#### Slide 02 - Our journey

- **Audience move**: Unclear what the internship covered → holds a seven-stage map of the journey and the five acts
- **Relationships**: order — organisational immersion → field immersion → institutional exposure → problem discovery → market research → brand development → implementation blueprint; membership — stages grouped into Acts I–V
- **Title**: From understanding communities to building a market-linked opportunity
- **Core message**: The brand came last; understanding came first.
- **Content**: seven stages with one-line descriptions and act grouping · thumbnail photo evidence where available

#### Slide 03 - Understanding MVS

- **Audience move**: MVS is a name → MVS is a grassroots ecosystem with farmers, SHGs and field teams that a brand could build on
- **Relationships**: membership — intervention areas belong to MVS; order — our understanding moved from organisation profile → programmes (PRANA, PRERANA) → people and field relationships
- **Title**: MVS: an ecosystem, not just an organisation
- **Core message**: MVS's relationships with farmers and women's institutions are the foundation any brand must rest on.
- **Content**: Founded 1996 · Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Uttarakhand, Meghalaya · women empowerment, sustainable agriculture, water conservation, climate resilience, education, rural development · works with farmers, rural households and SHGs · PRERANA: farm and non-farm livelihoods for rural women · PRANA and PRERANA field and programme teams facilitated our field visits · how our view evolved (profile → programmes → people)
- **Images**: phx_s03_mvs_orientation.png
- **Fact IDs**: report §2, Acknowledgement

### Part 2: Act II — Learning from the ground

- **Core message**: Looking beyond formal meetings showed real capability whose reach is limited by market access.
- **Content**: community visits and conversations in village homes · a beauty-parlour owner building a local service business · a grandmother skilled in pickle-making who has supplied products for corporate events · insight: the gap is between capability, market access and sustainable income

#### Slide 05 - Fatehgarh Sahib

- **Relationships**: link — nursery visit and Akshay Sir's explanation connect to farmers and MVS's role
- **Title**: Fatehgarh Sahib: entering the agricultural ecosystem
- **Core message**: The nursery visit showed how inputs, stakeholders and practices connect before a single grain is sold.
- **Content**: Harit Punjab Plantation Nursery visit · Akshay Sir explained nursery processes, initiatives, stakeholders and outcomes · walk through poplar plantation rows · first exposure to farming practices, inputs and MVS's field role

#### Slide 07 - The economics behind cultivation

- **Audience move**: Sustainability as an environmental good → sustainability as an economic decision for the farmer
- **Relationships**: contrast — SRP vs non-SRP practice on cost, water and returns; link — farmer decision factors drive adoption
