# Sangat: brand strategy and positioning

Status: working strategy for the identity build. Everything here is positioning and voice; it makes **no product,
certification, farmer, traceability or impact claims**. Where a statement is my judgement rather than a
measurement, it says so.

## 1. The brief in one line

**Sangat is a premium Indian basmati brand whose quality comes from people moving forward together:
farmers, Manav Vikas Sansthan (MVS) and the households who cook it.**

Tagline (always exactly): **Sanjhi Soch, Sunehra Kal**
Hindi (supporting, not a translation lock-up): साँझी सोच, सुनहरा कल · Name in Devanagari: संगत

## 2. Discovery

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Core proposition | Exceptional basmati you can feel good about, because the people behind it are in it together. |
| Purpose | To bring the people who eat rice closer to the people and practices that grow it. |
| Personality | Assured, warm, articulate, quietly proud. A good host, not a salesperson. |

| Target audience | Affluent, urban Indian households; premium grocery and gourmet shoppers; people who notice design. |

| Must not read as | An NGO / charity product, a rustic organic label, an ornate heritage cliché, a mass-market staple. |

### Name map (word map from the logo-design skill)

- **Sangat** (संगत): company, togetherness, keeping good company; in Punjabi usage also a gathering of people.
  *Cultural care:* the word also names the Sikh congregation. The identity uses the general meaning
  (togetherness) and **uses no religious iconography** (no Khanda, no gurdwara or langar imagery).
- **Sanjhi** (साँझी): shared, held in common. Also the name of *Sanjhi*, the Braj tradition of hand-cut paper
  stencils, a craft of shared festivals.
- **Sunehra** (सुनहरा): golden. **Kal** (कल): tomorrow.
- Devanagari detail: in संगत the **anusvara** (the dot, ं) carries the "n" sound, and the **shirorekha**, the
  headline that runs along the top of the letters, joins separate letters into one word.

Intersections worth drawing (judgement): *a line that joins*, *a shared middle*, *a cut-paper craft of
togetherness*, *a single golden point of origin*. None of these needs a leaf, a farmer or a globe.

## 3. Competitive visual positioning

The skill's site reader could not reach competitor websites from this environment (DNS blocked), so this shelf
map is **judgement from category knowledge, not measurement**. Confirm on a real shelf before launch.

| Shelf code | Who (typical, India) | What it signals | Sangat's stance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maroon / red + gold foil, ornate frames | India Gate, Daawat, Kohinoor, Lal Qilla, many regional brands | Heritage, festivity, "royal" | **Avoid red/maroon entirely.** Gold only as a restrained line, never a frame. |
| Monuments, palaces, crowns as emblems | India Gate, Lal Qilla, Kohinoor | Borrowed national/royal grandeur | **No monuments, crowns or crests.** Culture through craft and script, not landmarks. |
| Bright yellow / red mass-market | Fortune and private labels | Value, abundance | Low-chroma palette; space instead of bursts. |
| Purple / navy + script | Tilda (international) | Imported premium | Not purple; type is Indian-designed, not script. |
| Leaf green, kraft, sprouts, "organic" badges | 24 Mantra Organic, Organic Tattva | Health, natural, certification | **No leaves, sprouts, kraft or badges.** Our green is near-black forest, a lightness the organic set doesn't use. |

**Open territory (judgement):** a dark, low-chroma forest-and-ivory identity with Indian-designed bilingual type
and one disciplined gold accent. Typographic confidence instead of illustration-heavy packs.

## 4. Distinctive brand assets to build (targets)

1. A symbol that can be recognised alone at 16 px.
2. One ownable gold detail used the same way everywhere.
3. A bilingual type system (Latin + Devanagari designed together).
4. A pattern or graphic device recognisable without the logo.
5. A consistent pack architecture (the same layout grid across 1 kg and 5 kg).

## 5. Voice

- Short, plain sentences. Specific over superlative. Never "best", "purest", "No.1".
- Speak about people and practices only in terms MVS can confirm (the site's existing copy is the reference).
- English first; Hindi lines where they add warmth (tagline, a single line on pack), always set in a real
  Devanagari face, never transliterated into fake-Devanagari Latin type.

## 6. Constraints carried into every phase

- No invented claims; specifications on packs are labelled **PLACEHOLDER**.
- Logos are vector, outlined from OFL fonts (outlining allowed by the OFL; see the type section in the guidelines).
- Must work in full colour, one colour, black, white/reversed; tested at 16 px, 32 px, pack and large format.
- The 3D grain on the website stays the hero; the identity frames it and does not compete with it.
